# Explanation: what a lay reader needs

Question: what must a reader understand to read a porkchop plot, and where is the reputable wording.

## What they need, in order

1. Planets move at different speeds, so Earth and Mars line up only occasionally. The gap between alignments is the synodic period, 1 / (1/T_earth - 1/T_mars), about 26 months for Earth and Mars. That is why the islands repeat.
2. The cheapest path is an ellipse touching both orbits (a Hohmann transfer), launched when Mars is ahead of Earth by a particular angle. Windows are the times that angle comes round.
3. Reading the plot: each point is a launch day (across) and an arrival day (up); the colour is how much energy the launch needs (C3, in km^2/s^2). Dark islands are cheap; the rims are costly because the ship must go faster or take an awkward route.
4. Arrival speed matters too: the contours show how fast the ship meets Mars, which sets how much it must slow down.
5. The best cell is not always the shortest trip: a slightly longer, cheaper trip often exists.

## Recommendation

An always-visible short panel in plain words with a worked reading of one cell, a one-line definition of C3 on first use, and the synodic period computed from the ephemeris rather than quoted. The wording is drafted from the sources below, paraphrased, not copied.
